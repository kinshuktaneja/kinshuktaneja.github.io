**About Me**

Hi, I’m Kinshuk. I’m a doctoral researcher in health economics at RWI – Leibniz Institute for Economic Research and the University of Duisburg-Essen, supervised by Hendrik Schmitz and Martin Karlsson. My research asks how policy shapes some of life’s most consequential decisions, and I try to answer that question with careful, credible evidence.

My PhD is part of HEPARD (Health Economic Policy Advice with Real-world Data), a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Doctoral Network funded under Horizon Europe. HEPARD brings together twelve doctoral researchers across Germany, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden and Romania. Its partners include the Universities of Duisburg-Essen, Oslo, Tilburg and Uppsala, Babeș-Bolyai University, RWI, and non-academic partners such as the World Health Organization and AOK. The network’s shared goal is to understand and reduce health inequalities in Europe by evaluating real-world policies with modern causal inference methods.

Within HEPARD, I work in the Family Futures work package, which studies how health and family policies affect parents and children. My dissertation focuses on reproductive policy, early fertility and how outcomes carry over across generations, drawing on evidence from the Netherlands, the United States and Norway. As part of the network’s training, I’ll complete research secondments at the University of Oslo and at AOK. This gives me the chance to work directly with Nordic registry data and with one of Germany’s largest health insurers.

Methodologically, I specialise in difference-in-differences and related quasi-experimental designs, including staggered adoption settings. I work mainly with large administrative and survey datasets in Stata and R.

I found my way into health economics at Erasmus University Rotterdam, where I earned a BSc in Economics and Business and an MSc in International Economics. As a research assistant in the Bachelor Research Honours Class, I worked with Sacha Kapoor on a project challenging a widely cited explanation of why Indian children are shorter than their peers elsewhere. I then worked on mutiple projects relating to health and health policy, including a paper on urban heat islands in India and my thesis on contraceptive rights in the US. My master’s thesis was later nominated for the Erasmus Diversity Thesis Award. I also worked as a research associate on The DEI Reset (Palgrave Macmillan, 2026), building a dataset of more than 590 corporate diversity actions across 171 companies.

My path into research also included time outside academia. I served a full-time year as IT & Strategy Officer on the 60th Board of EFR, the study association of Erasmus School of Economics. In that role I managed the association’s information systems, advised on long-term strategy and supervised the committees behind the Master Career Week and Erasmus Recruitment. I continue to serve on EFR’s Supervisory Board. Earlier, I worked in revenue operations at the HR-tech startup Equalture, an experience that showed me how much depends on turning data into decisions.

Outside research, I enjoy cooking, travelling, films, cricket and golf. I’m also learning German, one compound noun at a time.
